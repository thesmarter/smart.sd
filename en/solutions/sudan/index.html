Decisions to make before development

Settling these points early makes the scope, testing and handover easier to review.

Connectivity and daily operations

Test on the connections and devices staff use. Define safe retry and synchronization behaviour for interrupted work, and keep essential screens lightweight. Offline payment capability depends on the provider.

Stock and accounting

Agree on branch records, currencies, approval rules and report definitions. Reconcile a sample of opening balances before importing all business data.

Payment availability

Verify the selected local provider, merchant agreement and integration documentation. Test confirmation, refunds and reconciliation without assuming universal access to an international gateway.

Delivery stages

Each stage ends with a review or test before work moves to the next one.

  1. 01

    Operating-condition review

    Record branch work, devices, connection patterns, currencies, stock units and the reports that staff need to produce.

  2. 02

    Lightweight workflow prototype

    Test the essential screens on representative devices and decide which tasks can pause, retry or synchronize after a connection returns.

  3. 03

    Staged build and data import

    Build the agreed modules, clean a copy of existing data and reconcile a sample before importing full balances and stock records.

  4. 04

    Pilot and handover

    Run a controlled workflow with the responsible users, verify backups and recovery, then document deployment and support ownership.

Expected deliverables

The final proposal identifies which deliverables apply to your project scope.

  • A process map covering branches, roles, devices and connection-dependent tasks
  • Responsive screens tested against the agreed device and connection profile
  • Import templates, sample reconciliation and an approved cutover plan
  • Backup, recovery, deployment and support instructions

What to bring to the first discussion

You do not need a complete specification. Real examples are enough for us to review the scope with you.

  • The devices, browsers and connections used at each location
  • Branch, warehouse, currency and stock-unit rules
  • Sample opening balances, product records and existing reports
  • The selected payment provider's agreement and integration material
  • A named owner for backups, recovery checks and support

Questions about delivery in this market

Direct answers about integrations, data and local requirements.

Have another question? Contact us.
Can the system keep working when the connection drops?

Some tasks can use local storage and synchronize later, but offline support must be designed per task. Payments, identity checks and live external data may still require the provider's connection.

Can you integrate a local payment service?

Yes, if the business has an approved merchant relationship and the provider supplies usable integration documentation and test access. We verify confirmation, failure and reconciliation before launch.

How do you reduce risk when moving existing business data?

We define field mappings, clean a copy of the source data and reconcile a sample before the full import. The delivery plan should also identify the backup, cutover and rollback steps.
