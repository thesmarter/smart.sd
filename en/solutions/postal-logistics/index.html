A technology partner for a transformation project, not a software supplier

We start from a simple question: what would it take for a postal operator or delivery company to run like one connected platform? The answer is not a nicer screen. It is an unbroken chain — an address a machine can interpret, a shipment registered once, and an event read at the branch, the centre, the customer's phone and the executive dashboard at the same time.

So we work in layers. We begin by analysing current operations and their boundaries, then engineer the technical architecture, its modules and integrations, and we keep every operational decision tied to a clear technical one — because the gap between the two is where transformation projects stall.

And if you already run systems, full replacement is not the default assumption. Many engagements start by connecting what already works and replacing only what actively obstructs operations, in phases each team can use as they land.

Local and regional reality

What differs in Sudanese and regional markets — and how we design around it

Postal projects here are not a model copied from another market. These points come from what we could verify, and each is checked with your organisation during discovery — we never assume they apply to you.

  1. 01

    Descriptive addresses versus a national address

    Some markets adopted an organised national address with postal coding (such as the Saudi National Address run by Saudi Post), while others operate on landmarks and unnumbered districts. Before designing an address database we establish which structure actually exists in your operating area, and build the one that works with what you have — coordinates and delivery zones where no postal code exists.

  2. 02

    International tracking is not your internal cycle

    Where there is no national tracking platform, operators fall back on the UPU's global track and trace service, which reflects the international exchange status rather than how an item moved through your own branches and centres. Domestic build therefore starts from the internal event record for every stage, then connects whatever international exchange requires.

  3. 03

    Weak or unstable connectivity in the field

    Routes and coverage areas run on uneven networks, which makes offline-first operation a requirement rather than a feature for the courier app: work is captured on the device and synchronised when the connection returns, with priority and conflict rules written into the design.

  4. 04

    Legacy systems that do not talk to each other

    Many institutions run a finance, HR or inventory system that is years old and exposes no interfaces. Integration may mean a periodic exchange file rather than a live API, and we state in the scope what is genuinely reachable in your system before promising live synchronisation.

  5. 05

    Payment and collection sit with licensed parties

    Digital payment and collection rails in the Sudanese market are run by licensed banks and telecom operators, and are separate activity from postal operation.     Cross-border dispatch requires advance item data and agreed message patterns with partners. Building on known standards beats inventing a private encoding

The postal sector runs on internationally agreed tracking numbers, messages and addresses. Before designing any unit we establish which standard or product you use, or must interoperate with, and build the right interface instead of a private layer that is hard to connect later.

  • Tracking numbers and barcodes

    Tracking-number check digits and barcode generation follow international postal specifications (such as UPU S10), so an item stays readable inside your network and in cross-border exchange.

  • Address format

    National address structures and their exchange are described by standards such as UPU S42, aligned with ISO 19160-4 — the basis for any address database that must work with a validation service or government gateway.

  • Item data and events

    Postal message exchange guides (such as ITMREF and REFRSP) and item information services (such as Postal-4i) define what each event should carry — which is what makes a movement history shareable with a partner or an oversight body.

  • Advance data and customs

    International dispatch depends on electronic advance data about contents and customs declarations. Integration with these is planned during architecture, alongside the authorities and systems cleared on the client side.

  • Supply-chain identifiers

    GS1 keys such as GLN, SSCC and GIAI give sites, handling units and pallets one shared identity — valuable when you work across warehouses, partners or ERP systems.

  • Cybersecurity

    Protection, monitoring and response controls are designed with reference to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework in its current 2.0 edition, which also has an official Arabic translation — used as a design reference, not claimed as a certificate.

  • Conformity and certification

    Interoperability and security conformance in the postal sector is carried out by specialist bodies such as UPU-TechCert. If your institution needs that kind of attestation, we design and document for it — we do not issue it ourselves.

The questions asked before a postal platform is contracted

Direct answers, each naming what we would need from you to say something precise.

Something else on your mind?
Can you build a complete postal system from scratch?

Yes — this is how our enterprise work normally runs: a process map first, then the four layers engineered (infrastructure, core system, business services, customer channels), developed in batches that each become usable. The order of those batches depends on where your operation loses the most today.

Can you improve an existing system instead of replacing it?

Usually, and it is the cheaper, lower-risk option when part of your current setup works. We inventory what you have: data, available interfaces, code condition and who holds the licence. Then one of three paths — add a front channel to it, build new modules that connect to it, or replace a specific unit. Full replacement assumes every alternative has been examined.

Do the solutions support multiple branches and regions?

The design assumes a network: branches, centres, deposit points and routes, permissions per site, parcel inventory per branch, and documented transfer orders between them. What we need from you is your site map, service list and target delivery windows per destination so cut-offs and states can be set realistically.

Can you build apps for customers and for couriers?

Yes — a customer app for Android and iOS, and a field app for couriers covering tasks, scanning and proof of delivery. The field app is designed to work when connectivity drops: operations are stored on the device and synchronised later. That is a design decision rather than a default, so priority and conflict rules are fixed during architecture, before development.

Can letters and parcels be tracked in real time?

Tracking works through events: each scan or action updates the shipment record, which the customer and the dashboard both read. How immediate that can be depends on connectivity and hardware along the actual path — which is why we first establish where scanning is feasible, and whether sorting equipment or partner scanners exist, before promising what will be live in your network.

Can the platform connect to other shipping companies?

Yes, wherever a partner exposes an API or an agreed exchange file with rates, routes and status events. We normally build one integration layer that speaks to several providers, so adding a third carrier does not fork the logic.

Can it link to payment gateways?

Technically yes, once an eligible merchant account exists and the provider supplies interfaces, documentation and a test environment. Before building, we specify payment, failure, refund and settlement flows and how each lands in accounting. One important distinction: providing payment or collection services for others is regulated activity and stays with a licensed provider — our role is technical, not regulatory.

Can the systems integrate with ERP and CRM?

That is core to how we work; a postal system should not be an island. Accounting, inventory and invoicing connect to your ERP, and customer data and follow-ups to your CRM, through interfaces, webhooks and error logs. We ask early for a sample export and the provider documentation so nothing is built on assumptions about your systems.

Can you develop a portal for government entities?

Yes, and it is closest to the enterprise portal: multiple user accounts, internal roles and approvals, bulk dispatch, an auditable transaction register, and digital correspondence mailboxes for beneficiaries. Confidentiality, data-residency and classification requirements are set by the entity or the regulator in that country; we implement and document the agreed controls rather than certifying compliance.

Do the systems support Arabic and English?

Yes, as a design condition rather than a later addition: bilingual interfaces, correct right-to-left Arabic across screens, reports, notifications and validation messages, and content keys your own team maintains. Every screen is reviewed in both directions and in light and dark mode before sign-off.

Can you provide hosting, operation and ongoing support?

Yes. We run the platform in your own environment or in capacity provisioned in your name, as constrained by your regulator and your institution's preference, with monitoring, backup, tested restoration, updates, support and severity-based incident handling. Duration, coverage and service levels are written in the contract; we do not assume one operating model for every client.

How is the project cost determined?

After the discovery and architecture stages, because cost is a function of module count, integration scope, automation depth and network size — not of the system's name. For an honest first estimate we need: shipment volume, number of branches and couriers, existing systems, and which services you want digitised first. We normally propose it in phases so each one can be approved on its own.

Can the project be delivered in phases?

Yes, and for large networks we recommend it. The test for each phase is that it ends in something genuinely used: a tracking portal, then a courier app, then sorting and transfers, then the core system — or the reverse, depending on where the biggest loss sits. Phases are designed inside the complete architecture so expansion never means rebuilding.

What do you need from us before you start?

Four things are enough to begin: your current service process map, a list of sites and roles, samples of customer, address and shipment data, and documentation or trial access for any system we must connect to. Having these early is the difference between a precise estimate and a later amendment.
