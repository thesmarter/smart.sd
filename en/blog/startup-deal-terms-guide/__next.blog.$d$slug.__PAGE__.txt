d:Te76,<h2>Introduction</h2>
<p>For many Sudanese founders, building a product is the easy part. The real challenge often arrives when an investor says, <em>"I'm interested. Send me your terms."</em></p>
<p>Navigating the legal and financial intricacies of fundraising can be daunting. With the rise of venture capital in the MENA region, instruments like <strong>SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)</strong> have become the standard. But what do they actually mean for your ownership?</p>
<p>This guide breaks down the essential deal terms every Sudanese founder needs to know before signing that paper.</p>
<h2>1. The SAFE Agreement</h2>
<p>Introduced by Y Combinator, the SAFE has replaced convertible notes as the go-to instrument for early-stage fundraising in Africa and the Middle East.</p>
<h3>How it Works</h3>
<p>You don't sell shares today. Instead, you promise the investor shares in a <em>future</em> priced round (usually Series A).</p>
<h3>Key Components</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Valuation Cap (سقف التقييم):</strong> The maximum valuation at which the investor's money converts to equity. <em>Example:</em> If you raise at a $5M Cap, and your next round values you at $10M, early investors convert as if the value is $5M—getting more shares for their risk.</li>
<li><strong>Discount Rate (معدل الخصم):</strong> A percentage discount (usually 20%) given to early investors on the next round's price.</li>
</ul>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Founder Tip:</strong> Always negotiate the Cap carefully. A low Cap can dilute you significantly if your startup explodes in value.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>2. Equity &#x26; Dilution (الأسهم والتقليل)</h2>
<p>Founders often worry about "giving away" their company. It's crucial to understand <strong>Dilution</strong>.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Authorized Shares:</strong> The total potential shares your company can issue.</li>
<li><strong>Issued Shares:</strong> The shares actually owned by you and investors.</li>
<li><strong>Option Pool (مخزون الخيارات):</strong> A block of shares (typically 10-15%) reserved for future employees. Investors will usually ask you to create this <em>before</em> their investment, meaning the dilution comes from <em>your</em> stake, not theirs.</li>
</ul>
<h2>3. Valuation Methods in Early Stage</h2>
<p>In markets like Sudan where data is scarce, how do you value a company with little revenue?</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Berkus Method:</strong> Assigns value to risk reduction (Idea, Prototype, Team, Strategic Relationships).</li>
<li><strong>Scorecard Method:</strong> Compares your startup to similar funded startups in the region (e.g., in Egypt or Kenya) and adjusts based on team strength and market size.</li>
</ol>
<h2>4. Key Clauses to Watch</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Liquidation Preference:</strong> Determines who gets paid first if the company is sold. Standard is "1x Non-Participating" (investor gets their money back OR their share of the exit). Avoid "Participating Preferred" which lets them double-dip.</li>
<li><strong>Pro-Rata Rights:</strong> Gives investors the right to maintain their percentage ownership in future rounds.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>Fundraising is a marriage, not just a transaction. Understanding these terms ensures you protect your vision and your upside. As the Sudanese ecosystem matures, sophisticated legal structures will become the norm.</p>
