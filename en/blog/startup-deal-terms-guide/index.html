Understanding Deal Terms: A Guide for Sudanese Founders

Prepared by the Smart Team editorial team · Editorial review: November 2, 2026

What does this article explain?

A startup funding agreement defines ownership, conversion and investor rights. Learn common terms and the questions founders should review with legal advisers.

Introduction

For many Sudanese founders, building a product is the easy part. The real challenge often arrives when an investor says, "I'm interested. Send me your terms."

Navigating the legal and financial intricacies of fundraising can be daunting. With the rise of venture capital in the MENA region, instruments like SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity) have become the standard. But what do they actually mean for your ownership?

This guide breaks down the essential deal terms every Sudanese founder needs to know before signing that paper.

1. The SAFE Agreement

Introduced by Y Combinator, the SAFE has replaced convertible notes as the go-to instrument for early-stage fundraising in Africa and the Middle East.

How it Works

You don't sell shares today. Instead, you promise the investor shares in a future priced round (usually Series A).

Key Components

  • Valuation Cap (سقف التقييم): The maximum valuation at which the investor's money converts to equity. Example: If you raise at a $5M Cap, and your next round values you at $10M, early investors convert as if the value is $5M—getting more shares for their risk.
  • Discount Rate (معدل الخصم): A percentage discount (usually 20%) given to early investors on the next round's price.

Founder Tip: Always negotiate the Cap carefully. A low Cap can dilute you significantly if your startup explodes in value.

2. Equity & Dilution (الأسهم والتقليل)

Founders often worry about "giving away" their company. It's crucial to understand Dilution.

  • Authorized Shares: The total potential shares your company can issue.
  • Issued Shares: The shares actually owned by you and investors.
  • Option Pool (مخزون الخيارات): A block of shares (typically 10-15%) reserved for future employees. Investors will usually ask you to create this before their investment, meaning the dilution comes from your stake, not theirs.

3. Valuation Methods in Early Stage

In markets like Sudan where data is scarce, how do you value a company with little revenue?

  1. Berkus Method: Assigns value to risk reduction (Idea, Prototype, Team, Strategic Relationships).
  2. Scorecard Method: Compares your startup to similar funded startups in the region (e.g., in Egypt or Kenya) and adjusts based on team strength and market size.

4. Key Clauses to Watch

  • Liquidation Preference: Determines who gets paid first if the company is sold. Standard is "1x Non-Participating" (investor gets their money back OR their share of the exit). Avoid "Participating Preferred" which lets them double-dip.
  • Pro-Rata Rights: Gives investors the right to maintain their percentage ownership in future rounds.

Conclusion

Fundraising is a marriage, not just a transaction. Understanding these terms ensures you protect your vision and your upside. As the Sudanese ecosystem matures, sophisticated legal structures will become the norm.
