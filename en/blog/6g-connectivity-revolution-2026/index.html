6G and IMT-2030: research, standards and use cases

Prepared by the Smart Team editorial team · Editorial review: November 2, 2026

What does this article explain?

6G research is part of the IMT-2030 framework. Learn the proposed use cases, the standards timeline and how to separate research targets from deployed services.

Introduction to the 6G Era

As we settle into 2026, the conversation around mobile connectivity is rapidly shifting from 5G deployment to the dawn of 6G technology. While 5G brought us speed and the initial infrastructure for the Internet of Things (IoT), 6G promises to be the fabric that seamlessly weaves the digital and physical worlds together. It is not just an upgrade; it is a fundamental leap forward in how we interact with data, devices, and each other.

The development of 6G networks is driven by the need for ultra-low latency, massive bandwidth, and the ability to connect trillions of devices simultaneously (Internet of Everything). This technology is expected to support data rates up to 100 times faster than 5G, enabling applications that were previously the stuff of science fiction, such as high-fidelity holographic communication and real-time sensory internet.

Key Features of 6G

  1. Terahertz Frequencies: Utilizing higher frequency bands to allow for massive data throughput.
  2. Integrated AI: Networks that are self-healing and self-optimizing using native AI capabilities.
  3. Holographic Communication: Enabling 3D immersive meetings and interactions.
  4. Digital Twins: Real-time synchronization between physical objects and their digital replicas.

The Impact on Industries

The arrival of 6G will have profound implications across various sectors. In healthcare, it will enable remote robotic surgery with zero perceptible lag, allowing specialists to operate on patients across the globe. In transportation, it will provide the instantaneous vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication necessary for fully autonomous traffic systems in smart cities.

Furthermore, the integration of Artificial Intelligence at the network edge means that devices will be able to process data locally and intelligently, reducing the reliance on centralized cloud servers. This "Edge AI" combined with 6G's speed will unlock new possibilities in immersive gaming and augmented reality (AR), making the metaverse a seamless part of daily life.
